Is Your US Shopify Store ADA Compliant? Here's How to Check

US Shopify stores are one of the clearest litigation targets in ecommerce. In 2025 lawsuit summaries, Shopify-powered stores represented roughly 32% of tracked ADA website cases, while ecommerce overall represented about 77% of US filings. That does not mean Shopify is inherently inaccessible. It means US plaintiff firms know exactly where Shopify stores tend to break: apps, swatches, drawers, modals, forms, and promotional themes shipped at high velocity.

For a US operator, the goal is not a vague accessibility score. It is to reduce ADA Title III exposure on the live storefront by fixing defects in revenue-critical templates before they become evidence in a demand letter.

Why US Shopify stores are targeted so often

  • Theme reuse: one broken component can affect hundreds of product pages.
  • App density: reviews, subscriptions, search, bundles, quizzes, and popups add hidden WCAG failures.
  • Checkout-adjacent complexity: carts, discounts, account flows, and address validation create form and focus problems.
  • Visibility: successful US Shopify stores are easy to discover and test.

Most common issues on US Shopify stores

1) Missing alt text on product imagery

US Shopify stores often rely on image-heavy PDPs where alt text is empty, duplicated, or meaningless.

2) Unlabeled selectors and swatches

Variant swatches, quantity steppers, and custom bundle controls frequently expose no reliable name, role, or state.

3) Broken keyboard flow

Mini-carts, mega menus, quick views, and popup funnels often trap focus or hide active elements.

4) Weak error handling

Discount code fields, login forms, and account recovery paths often fail WCAG error identification expectations.

5) Low contrast in campaign themes

US seasonal launches commonly introduce pale promo text that fails readability thresholds.

Checklist for a US Shopify store

Step 1: Audit the live storefront

Test homepage, collections, product templates, cart, account pages, and every popup or embedded app on the production site.

Step 2: Run keyboard-only testing

Verify menus, swatches, drawers, modals, and filters can all be opened, used, and closed without a mouse.

Step 3: Validate form semantics

Every input needs a label, accessible errors, and a clear relationship between helper copy and the control.

Step 4: Fix reusable sections first

Patch theme sections and snippets before individual products so the same issue disappears everywhere.

Step 5: Monitor every app update

US Shopify stores drift out of compliance fastest through app installs and merchant-led campaign changes.

US lawsuit economics for Shopify operators

Many US Shopify teams discover accessibility only after receiving a demand letter. By then, the cost is no longer just engineering time. It can include attorney fees, settlement pressure, consultant work, and delayed launches. Even a relatively small claim can cost far more than proactive monitoring.

What standard matters in the United States?

In the US, WCAG 2.2 Level AA is the de facto standard for ADA compliance. That is the benchmark most often used in US audits, settlement language, and expert reviews involving online stores.

FAQ

Are US Shopify stores automatically ADA compliant?
No. Shopify is only the platform. Your live theme, apps, content, and custom code still determine compliance risk.

What should a US Shopify store test first?
Homepage, collection templates, product templates, cart, account pages, popups, search, and all checkout-adjacent flows.

Can Shopify apps guarantee compliance?
No. Apps can help with workflow, but code-level barriers still require testing and remediation.

How often should a US Shopify store scan?
After every major theme or app update, and continuously if the store ships weekly merchandising changes.
